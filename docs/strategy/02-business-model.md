# Business model

Last updated: July 11, 2026

## DECIDED / WORKING DECISION — One capability chain, two buyer lanes

```text
Nonprofits:                 Funding pipeline -> Proposal -> Funded-program operations
Government contractors:                        Proposal -> Program operations
Public agencies:                                        -> Select program delivery
```

Wellridge owns work only when it contributes material judgment, integration, coordination, compliance, quality control, reporting, or delivery accountability. It is not a pass-through shell.

## HYPOTHESIS TO TEST — Shared operating machinery

An estimated 70–80% of delivery can be shared across the two lanes:

- opportunity and funder research;
- go/no-go analysis;
- compliance matrices;
- proposal calendars and document collection;
- narrative and workplan development;
- budget-to-scope alignment;
- submission production and QA;
- implementation plans;
- deliverable, spend, and compliance calendars;
- stakeholder, partner, vendor, and subcontractor coordination;
- reporting, records, and closeout; and
- document control and lessons learned.

The percentage is a working estimate. Track actual template reuse, labor mix, cycle time, and defects before treating it as proven.

## DECIDED / WORKING DECISION — Lane A: nonprofit funding operations

Initial work may include:

- qualified funding pipelines and prospect research;
- grant readiness and evidence-gap analysis;
- selected proposal development;
- proposal compliance and production;
- award setup;
- reporting calendars and grant-operating systems; and
- bounded quarterly or 90-day funding-operations scopes.

This lane is expected to have shorter sales cycles, deposits, smaller scopes, and lower working-capital demands than public-sector delivery.

## DECIDED / WORKING DECISION — Lane B: public-sector proposal and program operations

Initial work may include:

- proposal operations for established government contractors;
- program administration and compliance as a subcontractor;
- stakeholder engagement and public education;
- training and cohort logistics;
- reporting and data coordination;
- vendor, partner, and subrecipient coordination; and
- selected direct-prime work where administration, engagement, reporting, or coordination is central.

The initial model does not depend on direct federal prime work in 2026.

## DECIDED / WORKING DECISION — Value-add test

Before accepting a prime or subcontract role, document:

1. the decisions and risks Wellridge owns;
2. the work its people perform;
3. how it integrates partners and deliverables;
4. its schedule, QA, compliance, reporting, and stakeholder accountability;
5. how margin relates to real work rather than pass-through; and
6. the solicitation- and contract-specific subcontracting rules.

FAR 52.215-23 examples of added value are contract-specific and contracting-officer evaluated; they are not a safe harbor. Limitation-on-subcontracting rules also vary by acquisition. See [market and contracting research](04-market-and-contracting-research.md).

## DECIDED / WORKING DECISION — Revenue engines

| Engine | Primary role | Margin/cash profile | Scale constraint |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pipeline sprints | Entry offer and near-term cash | Deposit-friendly; low outside cost; founder/research intensive | Volume and founder judgment |
| Proposal operations | Proof, relationships, and larger fees | Good contribution if inputs/revisions are capped | Deadlines, client inputs, win-cycle variability |
| Program-launch/compliance tasks | Growth and past performance | Larger fees; more coordination and outside cost | Working capital, contract risk, delivery bench |
| Bounded funding-operations task orders | Repeat revenue | Predictable only if quarterly scope is explicit | Retainer creep and employee-like expectations |
| Public engagement/event-linked work | Selective expansion | High billings but potentially heavy pass-through and low margin | Evenings, travel, logistics, cash, vendor risk |

## DECIDED / WORKING DECISION — Financial vocabulary

These measures must never be collapsed:

| Measure | Definition |
|---|---|
| Contract or award value | Maximum or authorized value; not necessarily earned or funded. |
| Gross billings | Amount invoiced, including any pass-through vendor cost. |
| Recognized revenue | Revenue recorded under the applicable accounting policy as work is performed. Professional accounting review is required. |
| Cash collected | Money actually received. It can lag bookings, work, invoicing, and revenue. |
| Direct/project cost | Contractors, vendors, and other costs directly attributable to delivery. |
| Contribution margin | Recognized revenue minus direct/project delivery cost; excludes Felix’s labor and fixed overhead unless expressly stated. |
| Owner compensation + profit pool | Residual available before Felix’s personal taxes and additional retained reserves; includes compensation for Felix’s labor. |
| True operating profit | Residual after direct cost, non-founder staff, overhead, and a market replacement cost or salary for Felix’s work. |

For SBA size purposes, receipts generally include subcontractor costs and customer-requested pass-through purchases under 13 CFR 121.104. That size-rule concept does not determine GAAP or tax revenue presentation. Obtain accounting advice for actual contracts.

## DECIDED / WORKING DECISION — Payment and capital model

- Nonprofit sprints: working structure of 60% deposit and 40% on delivery.
- Government/prime work: milestone or monthly invoicing as the contract permits.
- Do not front major vendor costs without liquidity, advance/milestone protection, or other contract safeguards.
- Track invoice acceptance separately from invoice submission.
- Track DSO, aged receivables, cash reserve, committed vendor cost, and signed backlog.

## HYPOTHESIS TO TEST — Cash timing

FAR payment rules commonly use a 30-day due-date framework and set a 15-day accelerated-payment goal for qualifying small-business situations. Those rules do not guarantee operating cash timing. A 45–60-day gap is retained as a planning assumption until Wellridge has its own invoice-to-cash data.

## DECIDED / WORKING DECISION — Entry sequence

1. Bounded nonprofit and established-prime work
2. First-tier subcontract delivery evidence and eligible ratings/references
3. Small local/municipal direct scopes aligned with owned capabilities
4. Larger or federal direct-prime work only after readiness

## EXPLICITLY OUT OF SCOPE / REJECTED

- Brokering work while contributing negligible value
- Counting pass-through billings as proof of economic health
- Accepting clinical, engineering, construction, or software responsibility without qualified providers and appropriate governance
- Percentage-of-award grant fees or prohibited contingent government-contract fees
- Unlimited retainers or unmanaged revision/input obligations
- Assuming certification, set-aside status, or buyer eligibility that has not been established
