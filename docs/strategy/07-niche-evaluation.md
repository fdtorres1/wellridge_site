# Niche evaluation

Last updated: July 11, 2026

**Status: HYPOTHESIS TO TEST — Ranked, not locked.** The legal name and top-level brand remain broader until paid evidence shows where Wellridge wins, delivers well, collects reliably, and wants to continue.

Funding announcements and public examples prove that programs exist; they do not prove accessible demand, Wellridge eligibility, a buyer relationship, or an acceptable margin.

## Rank 1 — Nonclinical behavioral-health access and community-program operations

**Status: HYPOTHESIS TO TEST — Strongest initial specialization instinct by a meaningful margin.**

### Why it fits

- Felix has direct operational visibility into intake, referrals, clinician capacity, scheduling, reimbursement, staffing, outreach, vendors, and reporting in a multidisciplinary behavioral-health practice.
- The value is operational and administrative; Felix does not need to represent himself as a clinician.
- The work overlaps with grants, implementation plans, public education, partner systems, reporting, vendor coordination, and stakeholder management.
- Public behavioral-health programs create planning, reporting, performance, collaboration, and compliance needs alongside clinical delivery. See [market evidence](04-market-and-contracting-research.md).

### Potential Wellridge-owned scope

**DECIDED / WORKING DECISION — Potentially in scope after contract, data, and partner qualification:**

- program implementation plans;
- referral-network and provider-capacity mapping;
- community outreach and public education;
- partner convenings and nonclinical training logistics;
- compliance and reporting calendars;
- aggregate or de-identified outcome dashboards;
- vendor and subrecipient coordination; and
- closeout and evidence files.

### Hard boundary

**EXPLICITLY OUT OF SCOPE / REJECTED:**

- therapy, diagnosis, treatment plans, clinical triage, utilization decisions, clinical supervision, or clinical case management;
- 24/7 crisis-line or emergency clinical operations;
- Felix controlling or overruling clinical judgment;
- unnecessary PHI collection; or
- automatic referral or contracting preference for Wright Wellness.

**DECIDED / WORKING DECISION — Safeguards:** use aggregate/de-identified data where feasible; apply minimum-necessary access; require appropriate contracts, BAAs, security, and insurance when PHI is necessary; and require a licensed clinical lead wherever clinical judgment enters the scope. Use arm’s-length partner selection and conflict controls for any Wright Wellness relationship.

### Initial wedge

> Behavioral Health Access Program Launch: implementation plan, partner/referral map, outreach plan, compliance calendar, reporting framework, and first-90-day operating cadence.

**HYPOTHESIS TO TEST — Possible initial task:** approximately $15,000–$40,000, commonly first pursued as a subcontract.

### Validation questions

- Who holds clinical authority, and where is the boundary documented?
- What data is truly needed, who controls it, and can the work avoid PHI?
- Is the buyer funding operations/coordination separately from clinical services?
- Can a bounded first-90-day task produce a reference-ready outcome?
- Does the work remain daytime, remote-friendly, and low-emergency?

## Rank 2 — Workforce and training-program operations

**Status: HYPOTHESIS TO TEST — Preferred adjacent program family, especially near behavioral health and human services.**

### Potential program families

- behavioral-health workforce pipelines;
- peer-support or community-health-worker training;
- internship and practicum coordination;
- nonclinical professional development;
- employer/provider partnerships;
- youth-to-career or adult reskilling cohorts; and
- scholarship, certification, and placement programs.

### Potential Wellridge-owned scope

**DECIDED / WORKING DECISION — Potentially in scope after qualification:**

- participant-recruitment systems;
- partner and employer coordination;
- cohort calendars and training logistics;
- instructor/vendor management;
- attendance and milestone tracking;
- communications;
- grant compliance and reporting;
- outcome collection and closeout.

### Primary execution risk

Participant support and case management can become a large recurring administrative loop. Wellridge should own the program-management backbone while funded coordinators or qualified partners handle daily participant chasing, case management, and technical instruction.

### Initial wedge

> Funded Cohort Launch and Reporting System: workplan, recruitment workflow, partner calendar, participant tracker, communications, reporting dashboard, and first-cohort implementation.

**HYPOTHESIS TO TEST — Possible initial task:** approximately $20,000–$50,000.

### Eligibility caveat

TWC Skills Development Fund evidence supports an active Texas workforce-training ecosystem, but businesses participate through specified eligible applicants and the evidence does not establish Wellridge as a direct applicant. Every funding/program path requires live eligibility verification.

## Rank 3 — General public engagement and community education

**Status: HYPOTHESIS TO TEST — Useful capability, not preferred launch vertical.**

The work fits Felix’s hosting, writing, storytelling, and relationship strengths. Public records show material scopes. It ranks third because delivery can require evening meetings, travel, public events, production, rapid response, detailed logistics, and vendor working capital.

**DECIDED / WORKING DECISION:** embed engagement inside behavioral-health, workforce, or other qualified programs before treating it as an independent vertical.

## Niche decision gate

**Status: DECIDED / WORKING DECISION — Specialization is earned when most criteria are true.**

| Criterion | Evidence threshold |
|---|---|
| Paid repetition | At least 2 paid engagements in the same program family |
| Proof | At least 1 credible case study and reference |
| Scale signal | 1 task or contract approaching or exceeding $50,000 |
| Delivery system | Reusable method, templates, controls, and contractor bench |
| Buyer path | Identifiable repeat buyers, partners, and procurement routes |
| Economics | Acceptable retained contribution, collection timing, and working-capital burden |
| Founder fit | Daytime/remote pattern, limited emergency/travel burden, and work Felix wants to continue |
| Independence | Delivery can increasingly operate without Felix holding every detail |

Review by the end of Year 1 or during Year 2, or earlier if strong paid evidence accumulates.

## OPEN QUESTION — Evidence to collect

- Which buyer role owns the budget for nonclinical implementation?
- Which primes already hold relevant vehicles and need the proposed work package?
- Which licensed clinical leads can own judgment without making Wellridge a clinical provider?
- Which workforce partners can own instruction and daily participant support?
- Which program family produces the best mix of repeatability, margin, payment timing, and founder fit?

## EXPLICITLY OUT OF SCOPE / REJECTED

- Naming the company after a niche before clearance and paid proof
- Using Wright Wellness as automatic evidence, vendor, referral destination, or related-party beneficiary
- Treating mixed clinical contracts as proof of a purely nonclinical opportunity
- Owning participant case management or crisis response without the required people, funding, authority, and systems
