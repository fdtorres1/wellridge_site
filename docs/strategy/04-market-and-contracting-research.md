# Market and contracting research

Last verified: July 11, 2026

This document records dated planning evidence, not legal, accounting, procurement, or pricing advice. Contract-specific clauses and current official rules control. Source IDs link to the [research register](../sources/SOURCES.md).

## DECIDED / WORKING DECISION — Federal market scale

- The SBA FY2025 government-wide scorecard reports approximately **$179.0 billion** in small-business prime awards and **$93.7 billion** in reported small-business subcontracts: about **$272.7 billion combined**. SBA describes this as nearly $273 billion. ([S01](../sources/SOURCES.md#s01), [S02](../sources/SOURCES.md#s02))
- The scorecard reports unique small-business prime vendors declining from **60,951 in FY2024 to 56,725 in FY2025**, a 6.93% decrease. ([S02](../sources/SOURCES.md#s02))

**Correction:** This supports “a large market with fewer unique small-business prime vendors.” It does not, by itself, prove market concentration. A concentration claim would require award-share or similar concentration evidence.

## DECIDED / WORKING DECISION — Thresholds and set-asides

- The standard federal micro-purchase threshold is **$15,000** and the standard simplified acquisition threshold is **$350,000**, effective October 1, 2025. Special thresholds differ for certain contingency, defense-support, and humanitarian contexts. ([S03](../sources/SOURCES.md#s03))
- For acquisitions above the micro-purchase threshold and not over the simplified acquisition threshold, FAR 19.502-2 says the acquisition shall be set aside unless the contracting officer lacks a reasonable expectation of competitive offers from at least two responsible small businesses considering fair-market price, quality, and delivery. Above the simplified acquisition threshold, the same section also requires a total set-aside when the two-offer and fair-market-price expectations exist. ([S04](../sources/SOURCES.md#s04))

**Operating implication:** thresholds and the Rule of Two create an opportunity structure, not an entitlement. Eligibility, responsibility, NAICS size, competition, solicitation terms, past performance, and buyer judgment still govern.

## DECIDED / WORKING DECISION — Subcontracting and real value

### Limitation on subcontracting

For covered full or partial small-business set-asides above the simplified acquisition threshold and specified socioeconomic awards, a service prime generally may not pay more than 50% of the government-paid amount to firms that are not similarly situated, subject to specified regulatory exclusions from the calculation. A similarly situated entity must have the applicable small-business program status, be small for the subcontract NAICS, and perform counted work with its own employees. Lower-tier work subcontracted by that entity counts toward the limitation. ([S06](../sources/SOURCES.md#s06))

**Legal inference accepted for planning:** 13 CFR 125.6 does not impose a universal SBA limitation-on-subcontracting percentage on unrestricted awards. Solicitation, order, agency, flow-down, labor, and other contract requirements may still impose specific performance rules.

### Excessive pass-through

FAR 52.215-23 bars payment of excessive pass-through charges where the clause applies and a contractor contributes no or negligible value. Its examples include beneficial subcontract management, source coordination, delivery coordination, and QA. A post-award change that causes subcontract effort to exceed 70% of the total cost of work to be performed triggers notice under the clause; a parallel trigger applies to a subcontractor’s lower-tier effort. Neither trigger is an automatic prohibition. ([S07](../sources/SOURCES.md#s07))

**Operating implication:** solution design, integration, schedule ownership, QA, compliance, reporting, stakeholder management, and risk ownership may be legitimate Wellridge value only when actually performed, documented, and accepted under the contract. This list is not a legal safe harbor.

### First-tier past performance

An eligible first-tier small-business subcontractor on a prime contract with a subcontracting plan may request a past-performance rating. The prime generally must respond within 15 calendar days; the request generally must be made within 30 days after the prime contract’s performance period ends unless a later deadline is negotiated. A procuring agency must consider an elected rating similarly to prime-contract past performance. ([S08](../sources/SOURCES.md#s08))

**DECIDED / WORKING DECISION:** preserve scope, deliverables, acceptance, timeliness, management, and reference evidence for every qualifying first-tier engagement, and request eligible ratings promptly.

## DECIDED / WORKING DECISION — Payment and receipts

| Topic | Verified rule or evidence | Planning treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary federal invoice due date | Generally the later of 30 days after receipt of a proper invoice or 30 days after government acceptance, subject to exceptions. ([S09](../sources/SOURCES.md#s09)) | Do not start the cash clock until invoice and acceptance requirements are understood. |
| Accelerated payment | Current FAR policy sets a 15-day goal for small-business contractors and certain primes that agree to pay a small-business subcontractor within 15 days after receiving accelerated government payment, after receiving the subcontractor’s proper invoice and required documentation, without added consideration or fees. It creates no new Prompt Payment Act right. ([S10](../sources/SOURCES.md#s10)) | Treat acceleration as helpful policy, not liquidity protection. |
| 45–60-day operating gap | Not established by the cited FAR rules. | **HYPOTHESIS TO TEST** using actual submission, correction, acceptance, prime processing, and collection dates. |
| SBA size receipts | For SBA size calculations, receipts generally include subcontractor costs and customer-requested purchase reimbursements. ([S11](../sources/SOURCES.md#s11)) | Keep SBA receipts separate from accounting/tax revenue treatment; obtain professional advice. |

## DECIDED / WORKING DECISION — DFW and Texas entry evidence

- Tarrant County reports FY2025 procurement expenditures of **$128,728,352.26**, approximately **174** publicly posted opportunities, and **168** contracts with $176,966,410.20 in authorizations/awards. Spend and award value are different. ([S12](../sources/SOURCES.md#s12))
- Arlington’s official FAQ currently says departments obtain at least three informal bids for purchases between **$3,000 and $50,000**. The same page says both “more than $50,000” and an anticipated aggregate of “$50,000 or greater” uses sealed solicitation, so the exact treatment at $50,000 must be confirmed. The page also references both IonWave and a transition to Bonfire. Recheck the band and portal before relying on either. ([S13](../sources/SOURCES.md#s13))
- Cross Timbers APEX at UTA offers one-on-one counseling and free workshops. Its separate Bid Match service searches more than 3,460 sources and is listed at $300 annually. The page is internally inconsistent about a 72- versus 75-county service area but includes Tarrant County. ([S14](../sources/SOURCES.md#s14))
- Permanent Texas VetHUB rules took effect **May 12, 2026**, replacing emergency rules effective **December 2, 2025**. Eligibility requires at least **51% ownership, management, and operation** by qualifying service-disabled veterans with at least a **20% service disability**, a primary Texas business location, and SBA-size compliance. Certification is not a general small/minority/woman-owned advantage. Do not imply Felix is eligible. ([S15](../sources/SOURCES.md#s15))
- NCTRCA SBE certification can be useful only if Wellridge is eligible and a target buyer or prime recognizes it. Certification does not itself place a firm on bidder lists, establish qualifications, or guarantee work. ([S16](../sources/SOURCES.md#s16), [S17](../sources/SOURCES.md#s17), [S18](../sources/SOURCES.md#s18))

## DECIDED / WORKING DECISION — Market-entry implications

1. Use small, bounded nonprofit and established-prime work to create cash and evidence.
2. Treat subcontracting as a learning and past-performance path, not a margin-free pass-through model.
3. Pursue local direct scopes only when Wellridge’s owned administrative/engagement/reporting work is central.
4. Validate actual price, margin, workload, and payment timing on each early engagement.
5. Keep behavioral-health delivery nonclinical and identify licensed governance before any mixed scope.
6. Recheck current rules, thresholds, funding, portals, and appropriations before a real pursuit.

## EXPLICITLY OUT OF SCOPE / REJECTED

- Treating public contract examples as comparable-company pricing or guaranteed access
- Presenting federal market totals as Wellridge’s addressable market
- Assuming eligibility for a certification, grant, set-aside, or buyer program
- Relying on an explanatory page when current regulation or solicitation text differs
- Using AI research as final legal, clinical, accounting, or contracting authority
