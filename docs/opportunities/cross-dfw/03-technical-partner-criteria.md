# CroSS-DFW technical-partner criteria

Last updated: July 12, 2026

**Status: HYPOTHESIS TO TEST — Default to an established transportation-safety/planning firm as technical prime or technical lead, with Wellridge holding a named, bounded engagement and program-operations workshare.**

Reconsider that structure only if the released RFP makes engagement/program management the central contractual result and Wellridge clears every direct-prime gate. This document defines partner archetypes; it does not name, endorse, contact, shortlist, or authorize discussion with any firm.

This is internal planning, not engineering, safety, legal, procurement, licensing, federal-compliance, security, insurance, or accounting advice. Credentials, terms, and flow-downs must be verified against the released solicitation and qualified human review.

## HYPOTHESIS TO TEST — Required technical team

The ideal technical lead is not merely a general civil engineer or technology reseller. Subject to the released scope, the team may need to own:

- school-zone, pedestrian, vulnerable-road-user, and traffic-safety analysis;
- repeatable field inventories and data collection across the preliminary 200–300-school-zone universe or the RFP-defined sample;
- traffic, speed, crash, roadway, GIS, and statistical methods and QA;
- crossing-guard program criteria and technically validated city/ISD implementation guidance;
- technology-neutral driver-awareness requirements and systems integration;
- temporary technology-demonstration planning, installation, operation, and field safety;
- statistically defensible baseline and before-and-after evaluation;
- technical recommendations, professional review, and any required signature or seal; and
- sufficient federal-award competence to identify and comply with requirements actually incorporated into the contract.

## DECIDED / WORKING DECISION — Hard gates

A failure overrides the weighted score:

1. qualified technical authority and any mandatory license/credential are not available;
2. the team cannot demonstrate a credible method and capacity for the RFP-defined school-zone, field, GIS, and analytical scope;
3. before-and-after evaluation is not independent, technically defensible, and separated from product sales claims;
4. the firm is primarily a reseller/installer and cannot disclose manufacturer or financial incentives;
5. the proposed technology precedes requirements, site, safety, data, and evaluation definition;
6. data ownership, exports, retention, security, interoperability, and device lifecycle cannot be explained;
7. fieldwork near schools, students, or active roadways lacks qualified safety leadership and stop-work control;
8. the plan collects student, biometric, video, plate, geolocation, or other identifiable data without demonstrated necessity, authority, and controls;
9. debarment, exclusion, material conflict, misrepresentation, adverse cost/audit history, or procurement-integrity concern is unresolved;
10. RFP-specific insurance, professional liability, cyber, subcontract, or contract terms cannot be satisfied;
11. the firm refuses a written workshare, meaningful Wellridge deliverables, transparent budget, source access, prompt payment, or accurate reference rights;
12. Wellridge would be nominal participation, a cosmetic prime, or the financer of equipment, installation, or technical delivery;
13. engineering, technology, data, field-safety, cost-certification, warranty, or uncontrolled indemnity is shifted to Wellridge;
14. the firm treats the $6.25 million overall program funding as consultant contract value; or
15. references reveal unresolved, material problems with safety, data quality, schedule, collaboration, invoice support, or change control.

## OPEN QUESTION — Detailed qualification tests

### Transportation safety and data

Require evidence of a method to:

- integrate RFP-designated high-injury, roadway-safety, school-zone, and jurisdictional data;
- inventory signs, flashers, signals, crossings, guard-program conditions, roadway features, and operating gaps;
- use repeatable definitions, field protocols, assumptions, missing-data treatment, and QA/QC;
- measure vehicle speed, compliance, or other RFP-defined outcomes before and after intervention;
- deliver documented GIS/data products with metadata and usable, nonproprietary exports;
- work across urban, suburban, and rural school-zone contexts; and
- translate findings into technically defensible, locally usable decision guidance.

### Technology demonstration

Require evidence of:

- a technology-neutral concept-of-operations and requirements process;
- interoperability analysis covering signals, flashers, communications, power, maintenance, and any crossing-guard interface;
- site selection, permissions, school calendar, installation, traffic-control, and field-safety planning;
- baseline and before-and-after measures tied to the stated safety problem;
- lifecycle cost, maintenance, warranty, removability, device/data ownership, and vendor-lock-in analysis;
- physical and cyber-resilience controls;
- screening for prohibited telecommunications/video-surveillance equipment when incorporated requirements apply;
- a documented process for determining whether domestic-content requirements apply to each equipment/material category; and
- evaluation independent of manufacturer claims.

USDOT describes SS4A demonstrations as temporary activities that measure potential benefits through data collection and evaluation, with before-and-after assessment and findings incorporated into the relevant Action Plan. That program-level guidance does not by itself establish the consultant's exact contractual obligations; the released RFP and agreement control.

### Federal and operating discipline

Require the partner to show how it would identify and manage requirements actually incorporated into the contract, potentially including:

- project-specific timekeeping, allocable/allowable cost support, audit-ready records, and internal controls;
- baseline, progress, financial, performance, and final-report support;
- subcontractor screening, flow-downs, monitoring, and change control;
- small/disadvantaged-business, DBE, nondiscrimination, accessibility, and language-access obligations;
- debarment, lobbying, prohibited-equipment, and other required certifications;
- equipment inventory, ownership, maintenance, and disposition; and
- scope, schedule, budget, key-personnel, and client-approval controls.

Do not assume every recipient-level SS4A term flows to every consultant. Extract the actual RFP, contract, grant reference, and incorporated clauses.

## DECIDED / WORKING DECISION — Workshare behavior required before any authorized teaming commitment

A future teaming term sheet should address:

- named roles, tasks, deliverables, hours, and fee by firm;
- technical and professional-liability ownership;
- proposal responsibilities, capture-cost ceiling, and no unlimited uncompensated production;
- prompt payment and no requirement that Wellridge finance technical delivery;
- flow-down review rights and access to source material needed for Wellridge deliverables;
- accurate permission for Wellridge to cite completed work and obtain a reference where contractually allowed;
- client communication, change, substitution, exclusivity, conflict, and withdrawal rules;
- no bid shopping or post-submission replacement without documented cause; and
- survival of confidentiality, data, records, payment, and reference terms.

- financial-capacity evidence appropriate to the expected field/equipment burden.
